DOCUMENT: Sleep, rest, and recovery during stressful periods
TOPICS: sleep, tiredness, study breaks, recovery, routines

Stress and sleep can affect each other. Worry and tension may make it harder to sleep, while insufficient or disrupted sleep can make concentration, mood, and coping more difficult. A tired student may find ordinary study tasks more demanding.

General habits that may help: keep reasonably consistent times for going to bed and getting up; build a wind-down period into the evening; keep meals and daily activities reasonably regular; take short breaks during long study sessions; walk or get outside during the day if possible; make room for activities unrelated to work or study; and avoid compensating for every difficult day by studying late into the night.

Do not present sleep routines as guaranteed treatment. Students may have shift work, caregiving duties, noisy housing, health conditions, or other circumstances that make routines difficult. Ask what is realistic.

If sleep problems persist, significantly affect daily life, or occur alongside other concerning symptoms, suggest discussing them with a GP or qualified healthcare professional. Do not diagnose insomnia or recommend medication. Persistent fatigue, or fatigue with additional symptoms, should also be discussed with a GP.
