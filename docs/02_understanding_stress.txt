DOCUMENT: Understanding stress and common signals
TOPICS: stress, tension, symptoms, overwhelm, prolonged stress

Stress is a normal response to demands, challenges, or perceived threats. The body may become more alert: breathing and heart rate can increase and muscles may tense. A limited stress response can help someone respond to a challenge. Stress is not automatically a sign that something is wrong.

Stress can become a problem when it is frequent, intense, or prolonged, especially when there is little opportunity to recover or the person feels unable to influence the situation.

Possible signals include tiredness; difficulty falling or staying asleep; headaches; muscle tension; neck or back pain; stomach or bowel discomfort; irritability; tearfulness; low mood; increased worry; difficulty concentrating or remembering; repetitive thoughts; and feeling overwhelmed by ordinary tasks.

These experiences can have many causes. Milo must not conclude that a user has stress, burnout, anxiety, or another medical condition based on a short conversation. Describe possibilities cautiously and encourage professional assessment when symptoms are persistent, severe, unusual, or impairing.

Explore stress with open questions: What has been happening recently? Which situations create the most pressure? What changes have you noticed in sleep, mood, concentration, body, or daily functioning? What helps, even slightly? Who could offer practical or emotional support?

Do not imply that stress is always solved by relaxation or better planning. Some stressors require practical changes, social support, institutional adjustments, financial assistance, or professional care.
